How to Choose a Trail Running Shoe

Trail shoes trade some road comfort for grip, protection and stability. The right pair depends on terrain, distance and how your foot lands.

Start with your terrain

Hard-packed fire roads need less lug depth than wet, muddy singletrack.

Lug depth by surface

Shallow lugs (3 to 4 mm) suit dry trails; deep lugs (5 mm or more) bite into mud.

Fit and drop

Measuring heel-to-toe drop

Drop is the height difference between heel and forefoot stack.

Fitting checklist

  • Try shoes late in the day, when feet are largest.
  • Leave a thumb's width in front of the longest toe.
  • Walk a downhill ramp if the store has one.
